## Overview

The Decision Arbitration Framework describes how conflicting outputs from multiple agents, systems, or organizational decision units can be coordinated within complex decision environments.

As multi-agent systems scale, different agents may optimize for different objectives:

* risk reduction
* strategic growth
* operational efficiency
* legal compliance
* financial stability

Without arbitration mechanisms, distributed intelligence can generate instability, escalation conflicts, and responsibility ambiguity.

The framework is not designed to maximize optimization alone.

Its primary goal is to preserve organizational stability, accountability, and governable decision structures under increasing system complexity.

## Structural Principle

Intelligence can be distributed across agents.

Responsibility cannot fully disappear.

Human judgment therefore functions as a structural constraint within distributed organizational decision systems.

## Role

This framework identifies where coordination, prioritization, and responsibility assignment are required between competing system outputs.

It distinguishes between:

* system-generated optimization
* organizational authority
* human responsibility
* irreversible decision execution

## Interface

Arbitration becomes necessary when multiple valid system outputs compete for execution within the same decision environment.
