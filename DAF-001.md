# When Recommendations Conflict, Who Remains Responsible?

**Author:** Xufen Tu  
**Role:** Independent Interdisciplinary Researcher

More than one recommendation can seem reasonable, yet the recommendations may point in different directions. For a person affected by the eventual choice, the number or apparent quality of those recommendations does not answer who stands behind the result.

This is a public research observation about responsibility under disagreement. Competing outputs can make uncertainty visible. They cannot, by themselves, assume responsibility for a consequential choice. People and institutions remain accountable for the decisions they present as their own.

The observation does not establish a universal way to resolve conflicting advice. It does not prescribe how to compare recommendations, assign priority, or build a decision system. The circumstances and consequences matter, and human judgment can itself be mistaken.
