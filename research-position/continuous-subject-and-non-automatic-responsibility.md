# Continuous Subject and Non-Automatic Responsibility: A Public Research Position

**Xufen Tu · Independent Interdisciplinary Researcher**  
**Public Citation Version: v1.0**

A record can continue after the circumstances in which it was created have changed. A name, account, document or system history may remain visible. That continuity is useful, but it raises a question: what can the continued record actually establish about the person and their responsibility now?

My research concern is the gap between a continuing trace and a continuing human subject. A record may help show that something was said or done at an earlier time. It does not, by its mere persistence, establish a person’s present understanding, agreement, capacity or responsibility for every later use of that record.

This distinction matters when information is copied across contexts. An old statement may be treated as a current position; an earlier authorization may be read more broadly than intended; a history of participation may be mistaken for continuing consent. The record has remained available, but the human meaning of that record requires care.

My current position is that responsibility should not be assigned automatically from continuity alone. Attribution and accountability depend on the particular act, its context and the person or institution involved. A trace can be evidence relevant to a question without answering the whole question.

This public position does not deny the value of records. It does not say that responsibility disappears when time passes, or that every change of context cancels an earlier commitment. It also does not propose a technical identity model or a procedure for deciding individual disputes.

The human responsibility boundary lies in refusing to treat preserved information as a substitute for present, context-sensitive judgment about a person.
