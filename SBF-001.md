# Human Judgment and Consequential Outcomes

**Author:** Xufen Tu  
**Role:** Independent Interdisciplinary Researcher

Automation may help people collect information and consider possibilities. Assistance with those tasks is different from taking responsibility for what happens to a person after a consequential choice.

The public research question is how people and institutions remain answerable when automated material contributes to decisions with real effects. A useful output can still be incomplete or misunderstood. The fact that it was produced efficiently does not settle whether its use was appropriate.

This position does not claim that every automated activity requires the same human attention. It does not identify a universal point at which a person must intervene, and it does not provide a method for making decisions. Context and consequences require careful judgment.
